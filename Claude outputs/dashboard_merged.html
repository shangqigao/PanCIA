Interpretation

Thicker slices are associated with lower Dice scores in 5 projects with statistical significance: PRAD (ρ=−0.28**), BRCA (ρ=−0.19***), CESC (ρ=−0.18**), KIRC (ρ=−0.12***), LIHC (ρ=−0.09**). This is consistent with the 2D vs 3D model dynamic: BiomedParse segments each axial slice independently; thick slices (5mm) give coarser volumetric coverage and less context for VoxTell's 3D reconstruction, widening spatial disagreement. ESCA (218 slices, 1.9mm spacing) has near-zero Dice despite thin slices, indicating a task-difficulty effect rather than a scan-quality effect.

Key Findings

After correcting both the T-stage encoding and data joins, VT performs as well or better than BP in most cancer types. VT volume significantly outperforms BP volume in BRCA (ρ=+0.175*** vs +0.085*), KICH (ρ=+0.697** vs +0.216 ns), KIRC (ρ=+0.217*** vs +0.176***), ESCA (ρ=+0.336** vs −0.020 ns), LIHC (ρ=+0.198*** vs +0.174***), LUSC (ρ=+0.201* vs +0.056 ns), and STAD (ρ=+0.360*** vs +0.182*). BP volume leads only in CESC (ρ=+0.416*** vs VT +0.212***), where cervical tumour volume on axial MR is captured well by 2D slice-wise detection. PRAD (n=120) shows non-significant correlations for all metrics (all |ρ|<0.15, p>0.12) — expected, since prostate T-stage reflects extracapsular extension rather than linear tumour size. OV and UCEC have no AJCC T-stage in TCGA and are excluded from this analysis. Among invasion-staged cancers, COAD (n=23) shows the strongest associations for both models (BP volume ρ=+0.617**, VT volume ρ=+0.644**), while BLCA and ESCA show weak or divergent patterns.

VoxTell (3D) Segmentation Fragmentation

VoxTell produces multiple disconnected components per scan — a known artefact of 3D reconstruction from sparse axial images. Each connected component is labelled with scipy.ndimage.label. Qualifying: components with axial diameter ≥10mm (RECIST 1.1 measurability threshold). Across all 4,968 scans: median components = 6, mean = 40.3; 1,190 scans (24.0%) have no qualifying RECIST component, meaning VT produced only noise fragments <10mm in those cases.

Clinical Significance

VoxTell's fragmentation directly explains its poor Dice scores (median 0.000 overall). The 3D model detects many small spurious regions rather than one coherent tumour volume, consistent with the difficulty of 3D reconstruction from non-contiguous axial slices. STAD (stomach, median 49 components) and BRCA (breast, median 33.5) have the highest fragmentation — likely due to heterogeneous tissue and complex geometry. UCEC (uterine) is an exception: median 1 component, yet 46.5% have no qualifying RECIST lesion (171/368), suggesting VT finds something but consistently below the 10mm threshold. For KIRC (kidney clear-cell), 30.3% of 1,124 scans have no qualifying VT component (n=341) — by far the largest absolute count. After RECIST filtering, VT's median axial diameter falls far below BP's in most cancer types: OV (27.9 vs 79.3 mm), UCEC (16.6 vs 64.2 mm), PRAD (15.2 vs 59.5 mm), and ESCA (17.0 vs 87.4 mm). These gaps confirm that heavy fragmentation, rather than genuine biological differences, drives the measured size discordance in those cancer types.

Key Findings

Summary of Disagreement Patterns

Cancer Type

Worst: TCGA-ESCA (esophageal, 98% no-overlap, near-zero Dice), TCGA-PRAD (prostate, 78%), TCGA-UCEC (uterine, 77%), TCGA-OV (ovarian, 72%).

Best: TCGA-LUSC (lung squamous, mean Dice 0.285), TCGA-COAD (colon, 0.214), TCGA-CESC (cervical, 0.177). Lung and cervical sites are the most tractable for both models.

Modality

CT and MR show nearly identical spatial agreement (mean Dice 0.122 vs 0.106). The prior finding that MR disagreed far more than CT was an artefact of the size proxy. Both modalities have universally poor spatial overlap with median Dice = 0 in both.

Gating Recommendation

Only 310 series (6.2%) achieve Dice ≥ 0.6. Dice ≥ 0.8 (excellent): 101 series (2.0%), concentrated in LUSC, LIHC, and BRCA. Voxel-wise Dice is the only reliable gating criterion for spatial overlap.
