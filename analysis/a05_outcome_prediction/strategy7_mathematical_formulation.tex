\section{Hierarchical Bayesian Survival Mixture with Amortized Expert Selection}
\label{sec:strategy7}

\subsection{Problem setting}

For patient $i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$, let $X_i^R$ and $X_i^P$ denote the
radiomic and pathomic foundation-model embeddings, respectively. The observed
survival outcome is $Y_i=(T_i,E_i)$, where $T_i\geq 0$ is the observed time and
$E_i\in\{0,1\}$ is the event indicator. We consider three candidate experts,
indexed by
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}=\{R,P,RP\},
\end{equation}
corresponding to radiomics, pathomics, and their multimodal fusion. A latent
variable $Z_i\in\mathcal{K}$ indicates which expert is responsible for patient
$i$. The objective is to learn both a survival model and a deployable,
patient-specific selection rule without modelling the high-dimensional
embedding distribution $p(X)$.

The expert representations are deterministic functions of the observed
embeddings:
\begin{align}
    H_i^R &= f_R(X_i^R;\omega_R), \\
    H_i^P &= f_P(X_i^P;\omega_P), \\
    H_i^{RP} &= f_{RP}([X_i^R,X_i^P];\omega_{RP}).
\end{align}
The encoder parameters $\omega=\{\omega_R,\omega_P,\omega_{RP}\}$ are treated
as point estimates. Before Bayesian inference, each representation coordinate
is scaled by its training root-mean-square magnitude,
\begin{equation}
    s_{kd}=\left\{N^{-1}\sum_i H_{ikd}^2\right\}^{1/2},
    \qquad \widetilde H_{ikd}=\frac{H_{ikd}}{s_{kd}},
\end{equation}
with the coefficient reparameterization
$\widetilde\beta_{kd}=s_{kd}\beta_{kd}$. Consequently,
$\widetilde H_i^{k\top}\widetilde\beta_k=H_i^{k\top}\beta_k$ exactly. No
expert-specific centering is applied, so the shared-baseline interpretation is
preserved.

\subsection{Conditional graphical model}

Let $\rho=(\rho_R,\rho_P,\rho_{RP})$ denote the population proportions of the
three experts. Conditional on the observed embeddings, the model factorizes as
\begin{align}
&p(\rho,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_0,
        \boldsymbol{Z},\boldsymbol{Y}\mid\boldsymbol{X}) \nonumber\\
&\quad =p(\rho)\,p(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_0)
    \prod_{k\in\mathcal K}p(\beta_k)
    \prod_{i=1}^{N}
    p(Z_i\mid\rho)
    p(Y_i\mid X_i,Z_i,\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_0),
\label{eq:conditional-factorization}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_k:k\in\mathcal K\}$ and
$\boldsymbol{\lambda}_0=(\lambda_{01},\ldots,\lambda_{0J})$. The corresponding
graphical model is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:strategy7-pgm}. The router is an
amortized inference model and is therefore represented separately from the
conditional generative model.

\subsection{Piecewise-exponential survival experts}

Time is partitioned into $J$ intervals by
$0=b_0<b_1<\cdots<b_J$. Expert $k$ assigns patient $i$ the log-risk
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{ik}=\widetilde H_i^{k\top}\beta_k.
\end{equation}
Within interval $j$, its hazard is
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_{ik}(t)=\lambda_{0j}\exp(\eta_{ik}),
    \qquad b_{j-1}\leq t<b_j,
\end{equation}
where the baseline hazard $\lambda_{0j}$ is shared by all experts. Sharing the
baseline prevents expert assignment from being driven by arbitrary differences
in baseline calibration.

Let
\begin{equation}
    a_{ij}=\max\!\left\{0,\min(T_i,b_j)-b_{j-1}\right\}
\end{equation}
be the exposure of patient $i$ in interval $j$, and let $j(i)$ denote the
interval containing $T_i$. The individual expert log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{ik}
    =E_i\left\{\log\lambda_{0,j(i)}+\eta_{ik}\right\}
    -\exp(\eta_{ik})\sum_{j=1}^{J}a_{ij}\lambda_{0j}.
\label{eq:piecewise-loglik}
\end{equation}
Consequently, both events and right-censored observations contribute a proper
individual likelihood.

The Bayesian survival heads use the priors
\begin{align}
    \beta_k &\sim \mathcal N(0,\tau_\beta^2 I), \\
    \log\lambda_{0j} &\sim \mathcal N(\mu_0,\tau_0^2).
\end{align}

\subsection{Repeated-CV empirical reliability}

Repeated cross-validation is used to estimate population reliability and
training-set instability. For repeat $b\in\{1,\ldots,B\}$, let $C_k^{(b)}$ be
the out-of-fold concordance index of expert $k$. The reliability score is the
repeat mean
\begin{equation}
    g_k=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}C_k^{(b)},
\end{equation}
and the soft empirical reliability distribution
\begin{equation}
    p_k^{\mathrm{CV}}
    =\frac{\exp\{\gamma(g_k-\bar g)\}}
    {\sum_{l\in\mathcal K}\exp\{\gamma(g_l-\bar g)\}},
\label{eq:cv-reliability}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma>0$ controls the influence of reliability differences. The
between-repeat standard deviation of $C_k^{(b)}$ is retained as a diagnostic
but does not alter the population prior.

Each held-out prediction is expressed as log cumulative hazard at a common
training horizon $t^*$,
\begin{equation}
    \zeta_{ik}^{(b)}(t^*)
    =\log\Lambda_{0}^{(b)}(t^*)+\eta_{ik}^{(b)}.
\end{equation}
The fixed CV uncertainty is
\begin{equation}
    u_{ik}^{\mathrm{CV}}
    =\operatorname{SD}_{b}\!\left(\zeta_{ik}^{(b)}(t^*)\right).
\end{equation}
For a new patient, predictions are first averaged over folds within each repeat,
and uncertainty is then evaluated across repeat-level predictions.

Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:cv-reliability} is estimated from the outer-training
cohort rather than specified externally, it should be interpreted as an
empirical-Bayes prior centre or data-dependent regularizer.

\subsection{Hierarchical assignment prior}

The population expert proportions follow
\begin{equation}
    \rho\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\alpha_0),
    \qquad
    \alpha_{0k}=\kappa N p_k^{\mathrm{CV}},
\label{eq:dirichlet-prior}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa>0$ controls the effective concentration of the population prior.
Conditional assignments satisfy
\begin{equation}
    Z_i\mid\rho\sim\operatorname{Categorical}(\rho).
\end{equation}
For $q(\rho)=\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E_q[\log\rho_k]
    =\psi(\alpha_k)-\psi\!\left(\sum_{l\in\mathcal K}\alpha_l\right).
\label{eq:expected-log-rho}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Variational assignment responsibilities}
\label{sec:responsibility-derivation}

The responsibility is not itself a log-likelihood. It is the variational
posterior probability
\begin{equation}
    r_{ik}=q_i(Z_i=k),\qquad r_{ik}\geq0,\qquad
    \sum_{k\in\mathcal K}r_{ik}=1.
\end{equation}
To derive its coordinate update, introduce one-hot indicators
$Z_{ik}=\mathbb{I}(Z_i=k)$. Conditional on the observed embeddings, the
complete-data log joint can be written as
\begin{align}
&\log p(\boldsymbol Y,\boldsymbol Z,\boldsymbol\theta,\rho
        \mid\boldsymbol X) \nonumber\\
&\quad=\log p(\rho)+\log p(\boldsymbol\theta)
 +\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}Z_{ik}
   \left\{\log\rho_k+\ell_{ik}(\boldsymbol\theta)\right\},
\label{eq:complete-log-joint}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol\theta=(\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\lambda_0)$ and
$\ell_{ik}$ is the proper censored-data log-likelihood in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:piecewise-loglik}.

We use the mean-field family
\begin{equation}
 q(\boldsymbol Z,\rho,\boldsymbol\theta)
 =q(\rho)q(\boldsymbol\theta)\prod_{i=1}^{N}q_i(Z_i).
\label{eq:mean-field-family}
\end{equation}
The evidence lower bound (ELBO) is
\begin{align}
\mathcal L(q)
&=\mathbb E_q\!\left[
  \log p(\boldsymbol Y,\boldsymbol Z,\boldsymbol\theta,\rho
       \mid\boldsymbol X)\right]
  -\mathbb E_q[\log q(\boldsymbol Z,\rho,\boldsymbol\theta)] .
\label{eq:elbo}
\end{align}
It satisfies the exact decomposition
\begin{align}
\log p(\boldsymbol Y\mid\boldsymbol X)
&=\mathcal L(q)
 +\operatorname{KL}\!\left[
 q(\boldsymbol Z,\rho,\boldsymbol\theta)
 \parallel
 p(\boldsymbol Z,\rho,\boldsymbol\theta
   \mid\boldsymbol X,\boldsymbol Y)
 \right].
\end{align}

Holding $q(\rho)$, $q(\boldsymbol\theta)$, and all assignments other than
$q_i$ fixed, the terms of the ELBO that depend on $r_i$ are
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_i(r_i)
&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}r_{ik}
  \left\{
  \mathbb E_{q(\boldsymbol\theta)}[\ell_{ik}(\boldsymbol\theta)]
  +\mathbb E_{q(\rho)}[\log\rho_k]
  \right\}
  -\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}r_{ik}\log r_{ik}+C,
\label{eq:local-elbo}
\end{align}
where $C$ is constant with respect to $r_i$. Define the expected log-joint
score
\begin{equation}
 a_{ik}=\mathbb E_{q(\boldsymbol\theta)}
        [\ell_{ik}(\boldsymbol\theta)]
        +\mathbb E_{q(\rho)}[\log\rho_k].
\label{eq:assignment-score}
\end{equation}
To impose $\sum_k r_{ik}=1$, introduce a Lagrange multiplier $\xi_i$:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal J_i
 =\sum_k r_{ik}a_{ik}-\sum_k r_{ik}\log r_{ik}
  +\xi_i\left(\sum_k r_{ik}-1\right).
\end{equation}
The stationary condition is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\mathcal J_i}{\partial r_{ik}}
 =a_{ik}-\log r_{ik}-1+\xi_i=0,
\end{equation}
and hence $r_{ik}\propto\exp(a_{ik})$. Normalization gives the coordinate
optimum
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 r_{ik}
 =\frac{\exp(a_{ik})}{\sum_{l\in\mathcal K}\exp(a_{il})}
 =\operatorname{softmax}_k\!\left\{
 \mathbb E_q[\ell_{ik}]+\mathbb E_q[\log\rho_k]
 \right\}.}
\label{eq:responsibility}
\end{equation}
Because
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial^2\mathcal L_i}{\partial r_{ik}^2}
 =-\frac{1}{r_{ik}}<0,
\end{equation}
the local objective is strictly concave in the interior of the probability
simplex; Eq.~\eqref{eq:responsibility} is therefore its unique global maximum.

For $q(\rho)=\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$, substitution of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:expected-log-rho} yields
\begin{equation}
 r_{ik}=\operatorname{softmax}_k\!\left\{
 \mathbb E_q[\ell_{ik}]
 +\psi(\alpha_k)-\psi\!\left(\sum_l\alpha_l\right)
 \right\}.
\label{eq:dirichlet-responsibility}
\end{equation}
The final digamma term is common to all experts and cancels algebraically in
the softmax, but is retained to display the complete expected log prior.

The expectation over survival parameters is approximated with matched draws
$\boldsymbol\theta^{(q)}$ from the joint HMC posterior:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E_{q(\boldsymbol\theta)}
 [\ell_{ik}(\boldsymbol\theta)]
 \simeq\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\ell_{ik}^{(q)}.
\label{eq:expected-log-evidence}
\end{equation}
Importantly, Eq.~\eqref{eq:expected-log-evidence} is a Monte Carlo estimate of
$\mathbb E_q[\log p]$. It is not the posterior-predictive quantity
\begin{equation}
 \log\mathbb E_q[p]
 \simeq\log\left\{Q^{-1}\sum_q\exp(\ell_{ik}^{(q)})\right\}.
\end{equation}
The former, rather than the latter, is required by the ELBO coordinate update.

If $q(\boldsymbol\theta)$ and $q(\rho)$ collapse to point masses, the update
reduces to the classical finite-mixture responsibility
\begin{equation}
 r_{ik}
 =\frac{\widehat\rho_k
 p_k(Y_i\mid X_i,\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta})}
 {\sum_l\widehat\rho_l
 p_l(Y_i\mid X_i,\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta})}.
\end{equation}

An optional responsibility temperature modifies the update to
\begin{equation}
 r_{ik}^{(\tau_r)}=\operatorname{softmax}_k(a_{ik}/\tau_r),
 \qquad \tau_r\geq1.
\label{eq:tempered-responsibility}
\end{equation}
This maximizes
\begin{equation}
 \sum_k r_{ik}a_{ik}+\tau_r\mathcal H(r_i),
\end{equation}
rather than the original local ELBO in Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-elbo}. Thus
$\tau_r=1$ gives the exact mean-field coordinate update, whereas $\tau_r>1$
is an entropy-regularized, tempered approximation that discourages premature
assignment collapse.

The exact conjugate coordinate update for the population distribution is
\begin{equation}
 q^*(\rho)=\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\alpha^*),\qquad
 \alpha_k^*=\alpha_{0k}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{ik}.
\label{eq:exact-dirichlet-update}
\end{equation}
For numerical stability, the implementation damps the update of the normalized
population mean. Schematically,
\begin{equation}
 \alpha\leftarrow(1-\delta)\alpha+\delta\alpha^*,
 \qquad\delta\in(0,1],
\label{eq:damped-dirichlet}
\end{equation}
where $\delta=1$ recovers the exact coordinate update. Finite Monte Carlo
estimation, $\tau_r\neq1$, or $\delta<1$ converts exact coordinate-ascent
variational inference into a generalized Monte Carlo variational-EM procedure.

\subsection{Neural initialization}

Before Bayesian EM, the three encoders, point-estimate survival heads, and
shared baseline are trained without latent assignments. The equal-weight
pretraining objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{expert}}^{\mathrm{init}}
    =-\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
      \ell_{ik}^{\mathrm{MAP}}.
\label{eq:initial-expert-loss}
\end{equation}
An internal validation subset selects the training duration by the mean expert
negative log-likelihood. The experts and shared baseline are then reset to
their identical initial parameters and refitted on the complete outer-training
cohort for the selected number of epochs. The router is not trained during
this stage. MAP likelihoods from the all-data refit provide only the initial
responsibilities for the first conditional HMC update; they do not update the
population distribution. Router training begins after the first
posterior-informed responsibility update.

\subsection{Conditional Bayesian survival-head update}

After initialization, the encoders are frozen. Given responsibilities
$\boldsymbol r$, the compact survival heads and shared baseline have conditional
posterior
\begin{align}
&p(\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\lambda_0
      \mid\boldsymbol H,\boldsymbol Y,\boldsymbol r) \nonumber\\
&\quad\propto
 p(\boldsymbol\lambda_0)
 \prod_{k\in\mathcal K}p(\beta_k)
 \prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k\in\mathcal K}
 p(Y_i\mid H_i^k,\beta_k,\boldsymbol\lambda_0)^{r_{ik}}.
\label{eq:conditional-hmc-posterior}
\end{align}
The fractional likelihood in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-hmc-posterior} is the
expected complete-data likelihood under $q(\boldsymbol Z)$. We sample this
posterior jointly using the No-U-Turn Sampler. Posterior log-risk uncertainty is
defined as
\begin{equation}
    u_{ik}^{\mathrm{HMC}}
    =\operatorname{SD}_{q}\!\left(
       \widetilde H_i^{k\top}\beta_k^{(q)}
      \right).
\end{equation}

The conditional HMC step, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:expected-log-evidence}--
\eqref{eq:responsibility}, and the damped population update in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:damped-dirichlet} are alternated until the mean absolute change in
responsibilities is below a prespecified tolerance or a maximum number of
iterations is reached.

\subsection{Amortized router}

Responsibilities use the observed survival outcome and are therefore
unavailable at deployment. We learn an outcome-free approximation using
\begin{equation}
    \pi_\phi(k\mid S_i)
    =\operatorname{softmax}_k\{g_\phi(S_i)\}.
\end{equation}
Given posterior-draw states $S_i^{(q)}$, the router is refitted by minimizing
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{distill}}
    =-\frac{1}{QN}
      \sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
      r_{ik}\log\pi_\phi(k\mid S_i^{(q)}).
\label{eq:router-distillation}
\end{equation}
Thus, $r_i$ is an outcome-informed posterior assignment, whereas
$\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid S_i)$ is its deployable amortized approximation. They need
not be equal for an individual patient.

The deployed router uses the 14-dimensional state
\begin{align}
S_i^{(q)}=\big(&\eta_{iR}^{(q)},\eta_{iP}^{(q)},\eta_{iRP}^{(q)},
u_{iR}^{\mathrm{HMC}},u_{iP}^{\mathrm{HMC}},u_{iRP}^{\mathrm{HMC}},\nonumber\\
&u_{iR}^{\mathrm{CV}},u_{iP}^{\mathrm{CV}},u_{iRP}^{\mathrm{CV}},
D_{i,R/P}^{(q)},D_{i,R/RP}^{(q)},D_{i,P/RP}^{(q)},
Q_{iR}^{\mathrm{KNN}},Q_{iP}^{\mathrm{KNN}}\big)^\top,
\label{eq:router-state}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
D_{i,a/b}^{(q)}=
\frac{|\eta_{ia}^{(q)}-\eta_{ib}^{(q)}|}
{\sqrt{(u_{ia}^{\mathrm{HMC}})^2+(u_{ia}^{\mathrm{CV}})^2
      +(u_{ib}^{\mathrm{HMC}})^2+(u_{ib}^{\mathrm{CV}})^2+\epsilon}}.
\end{equation}
$Q_{iR}^{\mathrm{KNN}}$ and $Q_{iP}^{\mathrm{KNN}}$ are empirical percentiles
of the mean cosine distance to the $K$ nearest outer-training embeddings in
the radiomic and pathomic spaces, respectively. They are outcome-free and can
therefore be reproduced for a new patient against the stored training
references.

\subsection{Prediction}

For a new patient, let $S_*^{(q)}$ be the state computed using posterior draw
$q$, and define
\begin{equation}
    \bar\pi_{*k}=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}
       \pi_\phi(k\mid S_*^{(q)}).
\end{equation}
Hard deployment selects
\begin{equation}
    k_*^\star=\arg\max_{k\in\mathcal K}\bar\pi_{*k}.
\end{equation}
At prediction horizon $t^*$, its cumulative-hazard score is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat R_*^{\mathrm{hard}}(t^*)
    =\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}
       \Lambda_{*,k_*^\star}^{(q)}(t^*).
\label{eq:hard-prediction}
\end{equation}
For comparison, the posterior-coherent soft prediction is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat R_*^{\mathrm{soft}}(t^*)
    =\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
       \pi_\phi(k\mid S_*^{(q)})
       \Lambda_{*k}^{(q)}(t^*).
\label{eq:soft-prediction}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:soft-prediction} uses matched posterior draws and therefore
retains dependence between router probabilities and expert risks.

\subsection{Inference interpretation}

The resulting procedure is an empirical-Bayes hierarchical survival mixture
with conditional HMC and amortized assignment. It is not a fully Bayesian
end-to-end neural model: the encoders are point estimates, the CV reliability
distribution is data-derived, $q(Z)$ is represented by tempered
responsibilities, and only the compact survival heads and shared baseline are
sampled. Conditional on these approximations, each component has a distinct
interpretation: repeated CV quantifies training-set instability, HMC quantifies
within-model posterior uncertainty, the Dirichlet hierarchy regularizes
population assignment, and the router converts outcome-informed training
assignments into an outcome-free deployment rule.
